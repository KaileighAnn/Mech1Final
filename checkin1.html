Check-In 01.

Mechatronics I Final Project | November 23, 2026

Check-In Overview

Since completing my project proposal, I have finished all of the CAD work, completed the laser cutting, and assembled the mechanical components of my automatic dog feeder. I have also started developing and testing the Arduino code that will control the sliding gate mechanism.

My main focus moving forward is integrating the electrical components and making sure the feeder operates reliably.

Mechanical Design Progress

I completed the design of all mechanical components in SolidWorks. Each component was modeled individually before being combined into a complete assembly to verify the fit and placement of the parts.

Base and Side Supports

The base was designed around my dog's 8-inch food bowl. The side panels use interlocking tabs and slots to support the upper portion of the feeder.

Food Slide and Storage Container

I designed a sloped food slide with raised edges to guide the food into the bowl. The food storage container tapers toward the bottom to direct food toward the sliding gate.

Lid and Food Holder Support

I designed a removable lid for refilling the food container and a laser-cut top support to hold the container in place. The lid also provides an area for mounting the Arduino.

Sliding Gate Mechanism

I sourced an existing rack-and-pinion gate mechanism and modified it in SolidWorks to fit the opening of my food storage container. The servo motor rotates the pinion gear, causing the gate to slide open and closed.

Original Design: Automatic Sliding Gate – MakerWorld

Completed SolidWorks Assembly

After completing the individual components, I combined them into a SolidWorks assembly. This allowed me to check how everything fit together before manufacturing and assembling the physical prototype.

Manufacturing Progress

Laser Cutting

I completed laser cutting the base, two side panels, and food holder support. These components make up the main structure of the automatic dog feeder.

I used my previous laser cutting and fit testing results to determine the dimensions of the interlocking tabs and slots.

  • Material: Plywood
  • Average Material Thickness: 2.690 mm
  • Reference Slot Width: 2.60 mm
  • Total Laser-Cut Components: 4

3D Printing

The remaining mechanical components were designed for 3D printing. These include the food storage container, lid, food slide, and sliding gate components.

The gate components were prepared in PrusaSlicer using the following settings.

  • Printer: Prusa CORE One
  • Material: PLA
  • Nozzle Diameter: 0.4 mm
  • Layer Height: 0.20 mm
  • Estimated Print Time: 1 hour 47 minutes

Physical Assembly

After completing the CAD work and laser cutting, I assembled the mechanical components of the automatic dog feeder. This allowed me to check the physical fit of the parts and make sure the components were positioned correctly.

Having the mechanical assembly completed allows me to focus on integrating the Arduino, servo motor, and sensor into the feeder.

Electrical Components

The electrical system uses an Arduino to control the servo motor. I am currently using a push button to test the opening and closing of the sliding gate before completing the automatic feeding system.

Arduino Programming Progress

I have started developing and testing the Arduino code that controls the servo motor. The current code uses a push button to control the opening and closing of the sliding gate.

I am working on making the servo move at a slower, more controlled speed. The goal is to open the gate when the button is pressed and close it when the same button is pressed again.

Current Code Development

  • Control the servo motor position.
  • Use a push button to control the servo.
  • Adjust the speed of the servo movement.
  • Alternate between opening and closing the gate.

Planned Code Functionality

  • Read input from the bowl detection sensor.
  • Determine whether the bowl is present.
  • Automatically open and close the gate.
  • Control the amount of food dispensed.
  • Prevent dispensing when the bowl is absent.

Sensor and Servo Testing

I have started testing the servo motor using the Arduino and a push button. The motor responds to the Arduino code, but I am still making adjustments to its speed and movement.

The next step is to complete the sensor testing and verify that the servo can reliably open and close the assembled sliding gate.

Testing Objectives

  • Verify that the sensor detects the bowl.
  • Adjust the servo rotation and speed.
  • Verify that the gate fully opens and closes.
  • Test the assembled mechanism with dog food.
  • Prevent food dispensing when the bowl is absent.

Challenges and Design Considerations

Component Fit: The laser-cut and 3D-printed components need to fit together properly to create a stable structure.

Gate Clearance: The sliding gate needs enough clearance to move freely without excessive movement.

Servo Speed: The servo initially moved faster than desired, so I am adjusting the Arduino code to create a more controlled movement.

Food Jamming: The gate and chute must allow dry dog food to pass through without becoming stuck.

Sensor Placement: The sensor must be positioned where it can reliably detect the food bowl.

Power Supply: I need to determine how to power the Arduino and servo without keeping the system connected to my computer.
